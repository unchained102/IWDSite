<p>A strange wind blew through the town of Peaker&rsquo;s Blight. A west wind. The kind whose gusts carry a force such that you think it&rsquo;ll never stop blowing.</p>
<p>Peaker&rsquo;s Blight sat low in a valley where the desert swelled like a great red sea. Its streets were packed dirt, its buildings sun-bleached boards and flaking paint. Folks here lived slow and careful. Miners, ranchers, widows, and the sorts of men and women who found relief in small hopes and steady work. The town wasn&rsquo;t prosperous, but it wasn&rsquo;t dying, either. It existed&mdash;stubbornly&mdash;as if to spite the wind itself.</p>
<p>Robert Nash held his hat in one hand, squinting against the dust. He was familiar with this wind. The same one that carried him westward years ago.<br /> A common story, really: a young man with no prospects or relations to tie him down and a mind to see the world. He traveled&mdash;and eventually, as drifters tend to do, joined up with a group of outlaws. He learned to throw a rope and rustle cattle, to saddle a horse and make off with it.</p>
<p>And he learned to shoot&mdash;in fact, at one time Rob Nash&rsquo;s great pride was saying he was the fastest gunslinger this side of the Colorado, second only to the boss. The pride lingered now only as something wrapped in guilt, which knotted tight in his chest when he remembered those days.</p>
<p>The wind stopped. Silence. Stillness. He blinked, muttered something about the &ldquo;damn weather,&rdquo; and turned to head back into the Sheriff&rsquo;s Office.</p>
<p>He didn&rsquo;t make it to the door.</p>
<p>A young woman&mdash;Mara from the general store&mdash;ran toward him, breathless. &ldquo;Sheriff Nash! Trouble at the Silver Gull Inn. Some drunk&rsquo;s shootin like crazy, damn near knocked the sign off its hinges&hellip; says he&rsquo;s lookin&rsquo; for you.&rdquo;</p>
<p>Robert felt something old and heavy settle in his gut.</p>
