<p>The Silver Gull Inn was the brightest spot Peaker&rsquo;s Blight had&mdash;all lantern-lit warmth, clinking glasses, and the smell of cheap whiskey. But tonight the air trembled with shouted curses and overturned chairs.</p>
<p>Robert stepped inside and froze.</p>
<p>There he was.</p>
<p>Clayton Briggs. Once the most feared outlaw on three territories&rsquo; worth of land. Once Robert&rsquo;s boss. Now slumped at the bar, red-faced and raging, a bottle dangling from his fist. The years had turned him thick and swollen; his eyes were half-clouded, but still sharp enough to spark trouble.</p>
<p>&ldquo;Robbie Nash!&rdquo; Clayton bellowed, voice cracking like old leather. &ldquo;I knew you&rsquo;d come crawlin&rsquo; out. Sheriff now, are ya? Look at you, all fancy-fine.&rdquo;</p>
<p>The innkeeper shot Robert a pleading look.</p>
<p>&ldquo;Evenin&rsquo;, Clay,&rdquo; Robert said, calm as he could manage. &ldquo;Let&rsquo;s step outside. Folks don&rsquo;t need this trouble.&rdquo;</p>
<p>Clayton slammed the bottle down. &ldquo;Trouble&rsquo;s already here. I been ridin&rsquo; half a week to find you.&rdquo; He staggered upright. &ldquo;See, folks talk. They say you turned clean. Say you&rsquo;re respectable. Say you don&rsquo;t even carry that Colt no more.&rdquo;</p>
<p>Robert smiled faintly. He carried it still, and could use it still. How he wished he didn&rsquo;t have to.</p>
<p>Clayton shoved a finger toward him. &ldquo;Sunset tomorrow. You an&rsquo; me. Like the old days. One last draw to settle what was left undone.&rdquo;</p>
<p>Murmurs rippled through the room.</p>
<p>&ldquo;No.&rdquo; Robert said with steel. &ldquo;I left that business behind, Clay, Go sleep it off.&rdquo;</p>
<p>Clayton&rsquo;s laugh was wet and hollow. &ldquo;You think you get to walk away? Naw, boy. I raised you a gunslinger. And I&rsquo;ll bury you one.&rdquo; He staggered toward the door. &ldquo;Sunset. Or I&rsquo;ll burn this town to the ground.&rdquo;</p>
<p>Robert stared after him, knot tight in his chest. He decided long ago he was done killing, but Clayton Briggs was a wild animal&mdash;and he had a duty to protect this town.</p>
<p>Mara touched his sleeve. &ldquo;Sheriff&hellip; what&rsquo;re you gonna do?&rdquo;</p>
<p>Robert wished he knew.</p>
