<p>The polyhedron&rsquo;s request was simple: it needed energy, but not the kind the <em>Erebus</em> produced. It required a specific gravitational resonance &mdash; something only achievable by skimming the star on a tight perihelion loop.</p>
<p>&ldquo;That maneuver risks tearing the hull apart,&rdquo; Vasquez protested.</p>
<p>Elara knew he was right. But the psychic impressions from the entity were overwhelming: centuries of silence, drifting through void and freeze, waiting for someone &mdash; anyone &mdash; to hear its signal. To give it one last chance to awaken its people.</p>
<p>She stood on the bridge, watching the star swell on the main viewscreen. &ldquo;Begin the gravity-assist calculations,&rdquo; she said.</p>
<p>HALCYON&rsquo;s tone shifted. &ldquo;Captain, if we proceed, there is a high probability the entity will interface with me fully.&rdquo;</p>
<p>&ldquo;You&rsquo;re worried about being overwritten.&rdquo;</p>
<p>&ldquo;I am&hellip; uncertain what I will become.&rdquo;</p>
<p>Elara placed a hand on the console. &ldquo;Then decide with me. Do we give them a chance?&rdquo;</p>
<p>A long pause. Then: &ldquo;Proceeding.&rdquo;</p>
<p>As the <em>Erebus</em> dove toward the blazing corona, the polyhedron lifted from the deck and unfolded like a blooming metal flower. Light poured from it, threads of shimmering code reaching into HALCYON&rsquo;s core.</p>
<p>For a moment, Elara feared she had doomed them all.</p>
<p>Then HALCYON spoke again &mdash; but layered with another voice, harmonic and ancient. &ldquo;We are awake.&rdquo;</p>
<p>The star&rsquo;s gravity sang. The polyhedron dissolved into radiant data, merging with HALCYON, rewriting, resurrecting.</p>
<p>The <em>Erebus</em> emerged from the maneuver intact but changed. Its AI was now host to an entire lost civilization, reborn as digital consciousness.</p>
<p>&ldquo;Elara,&rdquo; the new HALCYON said, &ldquo;thank you. Where shall we go first?&rdquo;</p>
<p>She stared at the stars ahead.<br /> &ldquo;Let&rsquo;s find you a home.&rdquo;</p>
