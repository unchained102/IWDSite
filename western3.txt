<p>Sunset came dressed in copper and blood. The whole town seemed to hold its breath. Folks gathered along the main street, whispering prayers or placing quiet bets.</p>
<p>Clayton Briggs stood in the center of the road, swaying slightly, grinning like a man already halfway in the grave. His gun belt hung low. The old spark still lived in his fingers&mdash;the twitch of a man born for violence.</p>
<p>Robert stepped out of the Sheriff&rsquo;s Office. Colt sidearm gleaming in its holster</p>
<p>Clayton barked an ugly laugh. &ldquo;See, I knew you had it in you Robbie boy!&rdquo;</p>
<p>&ldquo;I&rsquo;m not a boy, no more clay, and I ain&rsquo;t gonna kill you today.&rdquo;</p>
<p>Clayton spat. &ldquo;Coward.&rdquo;</p>
<p>&ldquo;Coward or no I&rsquo;m here, what you gon&rsquo; do about it?&rdquo;</p>
<p>The crowd silenced as Robert and Clay circled&mdash;slow, steady. Clayton tried to focus on him, but whiskey blurred the edges.</p>
<p>Something somewhere creaked.</p>
<p>Clay drew.</p>
<p>Robert drew faster.</p>
<p><em>CRACK!</em></p>
<p>Clayton Briggs had but a moment of confused hesitation, looking at his former apprentice&rsquo;s upturned barrel before the Silver Gull&rsquo;s signpost smacked him square on the head.&nbsp;</p>
<p>He crumpled to the ground.</p>
<p>The crowd exhaled.</p>
<p>The pride hit him then, guilt free. &ldquo;I guess I still got it,&rdquo; he said.</p>
<p>Robert blew the smoke from the barrel, and deftly spun his Colt back into its holster.&nbsp;</p>
<p>He walked over to the unconscious man&mdash;still breathing&mdash; and cuffed him.</p>
